About

Research shaped by real environmental challenges.

Growing up in Rwanda, I saw how polluted water and inadequate treatment can affect daily life. That experience continues to shape my goal: develop rigorous, scalable remediation technologies that can serve communities facing water, soil, and sediment contamination.

I am a PhD candidate and Graduate Research Assistant in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Texas Tech University under Professor Danny D. Reible. My current work evaluates advanced oxidation and stabilization strategies for organic contaminants in sediment, with the goal of supporting safe, sustainable beneficial reuse of impacted materials. Earlier at Tianjin University, I studied reusable carbon-based adsorbents and sludge-derived biochar for hexavalent chromium removal.

Before joining Texas Tech, I gained industry experience supporting high-tech analytical instruments at Beijing Purkinje General Instrument Co., Ltd., including UV–Vis spectrophotometers, atomic absorption spectrometers, gas chromatography, HPLC, XRD, and laboratory washing systems. I also completed an environmental consulting internship focused on environmental assessment, pollution control, climate impacts, waste management, and recycling. My broader interests include environmental chemistry, contaminant fate and transport, resource recovery, sustainable waste valorization, and analytical methods for complex matrices.

Research

Selected research directions

Connecting fundamental contaminant chemistry with technologies designed for real environmental systems.

01

Advanced oxidation and sediment stabilization

Evaluating oxidation systems and stabilization approaches for PAH-contaminated sediment, with the goal of reducing risk and enabling beneficial reuse in construction.

  • Advanced oxidation processes
  • Contaminant transformation
  • Sediment reuse and sustainability
02

Reusable biochar for Cr(VI) removal

Studying activated carbon, sludge-derived biochar, and peanut-shell biochar across repeated adsorption cycles to understand performance, mechanisms, and site-energy distributions.

  • Adsorption and reduction
  • Waste-derived materials
  • Regeneration and reusability
03

Functional materials and catalytic treatment

Investigating MoS₂-functionalized sludge biochar and collaborating on persulfate-activation systems for efficient, durable removal of water contaminants.

  • Material characterization
  • Radical and non-radical pathways
  • Catalyst performance and durability
